How the tiers work

The tier is an evidentiary rank, not a quality score. It answers one question: how much corroboration does a claim from this source need before it goes in the report? A tier 3 newsletter can be more insightful than a tier 1 press release and still require more sourcing discipline.

1

Primary record

Statistical agencies, central banks, regulators, company first-party announcements, preprint servers. These sources are the event — a CPI print is not reported by BLS, it is issued by BLS.

Cite directly. No corroboration required.
2

Reference journalism

Wires and papers of record with published corrections policies and named editors. Reliable on verified fact; the risk is the single-source scoop that gets aggregated onward before anyone confirms it.

Corroborate exclusives before stating as fact.
3

Specialist analysis

Named analysts with real domain depth and an explicit point of view. Highest value per word on this list, and the best early-warning layer — they routinely surface things the wires reach days later.

Attribute by name. Never launder analysis into fact.
4

Institutional research

Working papers, Fed research, think tanks, multilateral bodies. Slow relative to a daily cycle, so treat as context and background rather than as the day's news.

Flag working papers as not peer reviewed.

Four domains, one desk

The list spans four domains because the story does. An AI capability release, a humanoid deployment, a robot-installation statistic and a capacity-utilisation print are the same story observed at different depths — and the economic claim usually only holds if you can see all four.

AI

Model releases, research, compute economics, policy. Fastest-moving and the most crowded with noise.

Physical AI

Robotics, embodied models, autonomy, industrial automation. Slower cycles, harder claims to verify — a demo video is not a deployment.

Economics

Macro prints, central banks, productivity, energy. Scheduled, revisable, and the layer that disciplines the other three.

Cross-cutting

Journalism, consulting, academic and institutional research that spans domains rather than sitting in one.

Operational caveats

Four things that will break a naive daily pipeline, found while verifying the endpoints above.

The wires are gone

Reuters and the Associated Press no longer publish open RSS — both return 401. Wire-grade corroboration now needs Reuters Connect or AP Newsroom licensing. BBC Business and IEEE Spectrum are the strongest open substitutes.

Calendar, not polling

US macro releases land at 08:30 ET on dates published months ahead. Driving a daily report off feed polling means finding out late. Drive it off the BLS, BEA and Census calendars with the template pre-staged.

Prestige does not imply a feed

The most-cited institutions are the worst-served. IEA publishes no RSS on any path tested; Stanford HAI, Berkman Klein, HBS, Kellogg and BAIR have none either. McKinsey is the only MBB firm with a working open feed — BCG and Bain both return 403. Budget for scraping, or lose the highest-authority layer.

Physical AI needs its own primaries

Humanoid announcements are announcement-driven and easy to over-read. Anchor them to installation data: IFR World Robotics and A3 order statistics are the robotics equivalent of a macro print. Neither has a feed, and both matter more than any demo video.

Some primaries block bots

BLS and the IMF return 403 despite being public. Use the BLS Public Data API instead of its RSS. Treasury moved its yield-curve XML; the old /system/files/126/ path silently returns HTML, not a feed.

EDGAR needs a User-Agent

Every www.sec.gov path returns 403 without a User-Agent declaring a contact email — verified: identical requests went 403 to 200 on that header alone. data.sec.gov is laxer, so a pipeline can pass tests and still break on the bulk endpoints. Set it globally and stay under 10 requests/second.

Deduplicate before you read

One announcement hits a dozen feeds within an hour, with tier 3 commentary arriving a day later on the same underlying event. Cluster by event, not by article, or the brief becomes one story told five ways.
